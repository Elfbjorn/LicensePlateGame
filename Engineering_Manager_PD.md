## Position Description — Engineering Manager (GS-14)

This position is located in the Executive Office of the President (EOP), Office of Administration (OA), Information Technology (IT), Enterprise Development Division.  
The incumbent serves as the **Engineering Manager**, providing strategic leadership and expert-level technical oversight for the design, development, modernization, and sustainment of enterprise applications across both internal and external environments.

The primary purpose of this position is to lead a multidisciplinary team of software developers and project managers in delivering secure, efficient, and maintainable IT solutions. The incumbent ensures architectural integrity, adherence to standards, and continuous improvement in software engineering practices.  
Although the incumbent possesses extensive development expertise, duties emphasize **strategic direction, architecture, and technical management rather than hands-on coding**. Work is performed across **unclassified and classified networks**, requiring adherence to security and information-handling requirements.

---

## Major Duties

**Technical Strategy and Modernization**  
Leads the organization’s technology modernization efforts by identifying, assessing, and recommending opportunities to improve existing systems and processes. Evaluates the full spectrum of modernization options—from on-premises optimization to cloud migration and re-platforming—and advises leadership on the technical and operational trade-offs of each. Works closely with product teams to translate conceptual prototypes into fully engineered, production-ready solutions using agile delivery methods. Recommendations may be presented informally or in formal briefings depending on scope and impact. Balances innovation and stability by introducing modern technologies and architectures while ensuring reliability, security, and continuity of existing enterprise services.

**Technical Guidance and Delivery Oversight**  
Oversees end-to-end software delivery by managing both project managers and developers to ensure that solutions meet scope, schedule, and quality expectations. Defines and enforces engineering and delivery standards while continuously improving development processes, CI/CD pipelines, and DevSecOps practices. Delegates detailed code and architecture reviews to technical leads but is accountable for maintaining effective governance frameworks, policies, and performance metrics across all teams. Establishes and maintains a center of excellence that documents proven patterns, reusable frameworks, and best practices to promote consistency across projects. Coordinates with both internal and external infrastructure and service providers to plan deployments, resolve integration issues, and negotiate feasible alternatives. Monitors delivery performance and intervenes when teams face challenges with throughput, quality, or technical foresight, ensuring projects progress efficiently and align with enterprise goals.

**Personnel Leadership and Development**  
Leads a multidisciplinary team of five to ten personnel, including both software developers and project managers. Establishes clear performance expectations and measurable outcomes through the development of formal performance plans, supplemented by recurring one-on-one meetings to assess progress and recalibrate priorities as needed. Conducts formal performance reviews, recognizes achievements, and takes corrective action where necessary to sustain accountability and high standards. Actively invests in the professional growth of team members by identifying both structured training opportunities—such as certifications and targeted courses—and informal learning resources including articles, books, online modules, and self-directed exercises. Balances empowerment with accountability, fostering an environment where employees can grow while maintaining alignment with organizational objectives. Addresses interpersonal and performance challenges promptly, whether technical or behavioral, and engages specialized HR or supervisory resources when appropriate to ensure fair, consistent resolution.

**Stakeholder and Cross-Functional Collaboration**  
Serves as a key liaison between the engineering organization and its internal and external partners, ensuring that communication flows effectively across technical, design, product, and support domains. Works closely with product and design teams to align implementation priorities with user needs and mission outcomes, while maintaining coordination with support teams to ensure operational continuity and issue resolution. Provides daily informal updates to senior IT leadership on project progress and risks, conducts formal weekly briefings summarizing major developments, and prepares higher-level presentations for executive leadership as required. Tailors communication to the audience—translating complex technical concepts into clear, outcome-focused language for non-technical leaders while maintaining sufficient depth for technical counterparts. Represents the organization in meetings with other federal agencies and vendors to coordinate shared initiatives, establish interoperability standards, and negotiate implementation strategies that advance enterprise objectives.

**Resource and Fiscal Management**  
Manages personnel and contractor resources across multiple subteams, ensuring alignment of staff, skills, and priorities with the organization’s mission and program objectives. Recommends and justifies funding levels required to maintain operational stability and advance modernization efforts, while adhering to established budgetary limits and fiscal policy. Participates in procurement and acquisition planning, contributing to the development, review, and evaluation of contracts, statements of work, and contractor performance measures. Works collaboratively with budget and finance offices to provide input, respond to funding inquiries, and validate cost estimates or resource adjustments. Actively identifies opportunities to improve cost efficiency through automation, cloud migration, and streamlined delivery processes, balancing financial stewardship with continued innovation and capability enhancement.

## Factors

### Factor 1 – Knowledge Required by the Position  
This position requires mastery of software engineering principles, object-oriented languages (Java, C#, Python), and modern web frameworks (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Angular, .NET Core 8, SharePoint, Apex). The incumbent must understand system, application, and data architecture; database design and optimization across SQL Server, PostgreSQL, and MySQL; and cloud computing in SaaS and PaaS environments. Expertise in DevSecOps and CI/CD using GitHub and GitLab, automated testing tools (Playwright, Selenium, Cypress), and containerization (Docker, Kubernetes) is required. The role demands proficiency in Agile methods (Scrum, Kanban) with Jira and Confluence, collaboration with product and design teams, and familiarity with generative and agentic AI tools such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, GitHub Copilot, and Figma Make. Strong communication and mentoring skills are essential for guiding teams and briefing executives.  

### Factor 2 – Supervisory Controls  
The incumbent operates under the broad direction of the Director, Enterprise Development Division, receiving assignments in terms of strategic goals and expected outcomes. The incumbent independently plans and executes work, sets priorities, assigns tasks, and resolves complex technical or personnel issues. Completed work is reviewed primarily for overall impact, policy alignment, and contribution to organizational objectives.  

### Factor 3 – Guidelines  
Guidelines consist of federal IT policies, OA technical standards, and industry best practices that often require interpretation or adaptation. The incumbent exercises substantial judgment in applying and extending these guidelines to hybrid and emerging technologies, creating new procedures and reference architectures that serve as models for other teams.  

### Factor 4 – Complexity  
Work involves multiple concurrent projects spanning diverse technologies—from legacy modernization to cloud-native applications—and requiring coordination across internal and external systems. Assignments demand balancing competing priorities, addressing novel technical challenges, and integrating policy, security, and operational considerations into unified solutions.  

### Factor 5 – Scope and Effect  
The incumbent’s work affects mission-critical systems supporting EOP operations and public-facing applications. Successful performance enhances efficiency, interoperability, and public transparency; failure could impede essential services or compromise data integrity.  

### Factor 6 – Personal Contacts  
Contacts include staff and leadership within OA and EOP, interagency partners, contractors, and vendors, engaged through meetings, reviews, and collaborative planning sessions.  

### Factor 7 – Purpose of Contacts  
Contacts are established to coordinate complex technical activities, negotiate implementation strategies, resolve conflicts, secure collaboration, and brief leadership on status and recommendations.  

### Factor 8 – Physical Demands  
Work is primarily sedentary, requiring extensive computer use and occasional movement between secure facilities.  

### Factor 9 – Work Environment  
Work is performed in both standard office and secure classified settings under strict access controls. Extended or off-hours work may be required for deployments or incident response.
