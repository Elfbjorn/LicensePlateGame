HITL Checkpoint Matrix

This artifact defines the human-in-the-loop governance model for Agile Blueprinting / Blueprint to Release.

The purpose of HITL is not to make humans manually redo agent work. The purpose is to make judgment, authority, accountability, and collaborative remediation visible at the right points in the delivery flow.

Working Principles

  • Use categorized, not classified, for ticket routing/status language.
  • Start with a universal, repeatable process. Off-ramps may be defined later, but they are intentionally not defined in this version.
  • A little friction up front is acceptable while the organization builds reliable habits.
  • Rejections return work to the prior stage, but remediation must be collaborative.
  • No reviewer should toss work back over the fence with vague feedback. The person raising the concern and the person responsible for remedying it should work through the issue together before re-review.

Decision Rights Model

This process uses a lightweight decision-rights model instead of classic RACI.

Role Type Meaning
Owner Person/pillar responsible for moving the checkpoint to completion
Approver Person/pillar with authority to approve, reject, request revision, or re-scope
Contributor Person/pillar expected to help remediate or clarify collaboratively
Informed Person/pillar who must be kept aware but does not decide

MVP Operationalization Scope

The full HITL matrix is the reference governance model, but it should not all be operationalized on day one.

For the MVP rollout, HITL governance should align to the first Codex skills:

  • prd-generator
  • prd-readiness-review
  • implementation-agent
  • unit-test-agent
  • e2e-test-agent

Everything outside those MVP skill workflows remains human-driven for now. Over time, additional workflows can be operationalized and the HITL elements can be modified as the process matures.

HITL Sign-Off Metadata

For now, sign-off should live in artifact YAML frontmatter. Later, this may be operationalized as a web-based process with a database backend.

Minimum HITL review fields:

hitl_review:
  reviewer_name:
  reviewer_role:
  reviewed_at:
  outcome: pending
  outcome_reason:
  questions_answered: []
  required_follow_up: []
  approval_record:

The YAML should answer:

  • Who reviewed it?
  • When did they review it?
  • What was their outcome?
  • Why was that the outcome?

Allowed Decisions

Default allowed HITL decisions:

  • Approve / ready to proceed
  • Reject / not ready
  • Approve with risk / proceed with risk
  • Needs revision
  • Re-scope
  • Split release
  • Not applicable

Rejection and Remediation Rule

When a checkpoint is rejected or marked as needing revision, the work returns to the prior stage. The reviewer who raised the concern must collaborate with the remedy owner before the work is re-submitted.

Examples of weak remediation:

  • "Needs clarification."
  • "Need to understand what happens when X arises."

Expected remediation behavior:

  • The reviewer and remedy owner meet or otherwise collaborate directly.
  • They identify the specific gap, ambiguity, or risk.
  • They agree what evidence, rewrite, test, design update, or decision is needed.
  • The artifact or work item is updated with the resolved detail.
  • The checkpoint is re-reviewed with the updated evidence.
